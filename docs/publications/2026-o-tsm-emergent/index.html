Abstract # ↰

In real-world deployments of multi-agent coordination tasks, agents can observe or communicate with their teammates only within a limited range, determined by their onboard sensing and communication capabilities. When robots cannot detect each other early enough, they may select the same goal and react only when they come within sensing or communication range, increasing collision risk. Yet coordination policies are typically evaluated mainly in terms of aggregate task performance, which does not reveal how agents adapt their behaviour as perception becomes limited. This raises a safety-relevant question: how does limited perception change the way agents coordinate? In particular, we investigate whether reducing the perception range causes a shift from predictive coordination, where agents can account for their teammates when planning their trajectories, to more reactive behaviour, where coordination occurs mainly in response to changes in teammate visibility. We further examine how this shift affects collective performance and collision avoidance.
